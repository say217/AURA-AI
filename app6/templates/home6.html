Introduction

Aura AI is a modular AI-powered medical imaging platform designed to assist in the early detection and analysis of cancer using deep learning techniques. The system focuses on histopathology and MRI-based image classification, providing diagnostic insights for healthcare professionals, researchers, and students.

It combines modern AI with explainable outputs, ensuring that predictions are not only accurate but also interpretable results. The platform is built using a scalable architecture powered by Python and Flask, integrating deep learning models such as ResNet-18 and ResNet-101. By incorporating explainable AI techniques like Grad-CAM, Aura AI enhances model transparency, which is critical in medical applications.

Objectives

The primary objective of Aura AI is to develop a unified system capable of analyzing medical images and providing reliable results with clear interpretability.

  • Enabling rapid screening of cancer using AI models
  • Supporting multiple cancer types and imaging modalities
  • Providing visual explanations to improve trust in predictions
  • Offering a scalable modular system for future expansion

System Overview

Aura AI follows a modular architecture based on the Flask Application Factory and Blueprint pattern, ensuring scalability, maintainability, and clean separation of components. Each module within the system is designed to handle a specific responsibility, including authentication, dashboard navigation, and AI-powered cancer detection.

The system workflow begins with user authentication, after which users are directed to a centralized dashboard. From there, they can access multiple diagnostic modules. Each module processes uploaded medical images, performs deep learning inference using trained models, and generates predictions along with visual explanations to improve interpretability and trust.

Currently, the platform supports several diagnostic capabilities, including breast cancer detection using binary classification, lung and colon cancer classification using multi-class models, and brain tumor detection based on MRI image analysis. These modules collectively demonstrate the system’s ability to handle multiple imaging modalities within a unified framework.

The lung cancer model is trained on a large-scale dataset consisting of approximately 250,000 images (L250000), allowing the model to achieve strong generalization across different tissue types. For breast cancer detection, the system utilizes the IDC dataset, specifically designed to identify Invasive Ductal Carcinoma through binary classification.

In addition, the brain tumor classification module is built using the Bangladesh Brain Cancer MRI Dataset, which contains a total of 6056 MRI images categorized into three classes: glioma, meningioma, and tumor. All images are standardized to a resolution of 512×512 pixels and were collected from multiple hospitals with the involvement of medical professionals, ensuring high-quality and reliable data.

This dataset plays a critical role in enabling applications such as image processing, deep learning-based classification, and predictive diagnostics. Its availability supports the development of accurate and efficient AI models for early detection and treatment planning. The dataset was contributed by Md Mizanur Rahman and published on August 5, 2024, with DOI: 10.17632/mk56jw9rns.1.

Core Modules

Authentication Module (app2)

Handles user registration, login, and verification with secure session management to ensure controlled access to the system.

Dashboard Module (app1)

Serves as the central interface where users can navigate between different diagnostic modules and manage their workflow.

Lung and Colon Cancer Module (app3)

Performs five-class classification using a ResNet-18 model trained on histopathology images. Identifies Lung Adenocarcinoma, Lung Squamous Cell Carcinoma, Lung Benign, Colon Adenocarcinoma, and Colon Benign. Outputs include prediction labels, probability distributions, and Grad-CAM visualizations.

Breast Cancer Module (app4)

Focused on detecting Invasive Ductal Carcinoma, this module performs binary classification between IDC Positive and IDC Negative cases. Provides heatmaps and clinical interpretation for enhanced understanding.

Brain Tumor Module (app5)

Uses MRI data with a ResNet-101 model to classify tumors into three categories: Glioma, Meningioma, and Pituitary Tumor. Achieves high validation accuracy, demonstrating strong performance on these complex medical images.

Explainable AI Integration

A major strength of Aura AI lies in its integration of Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping), an advanced explainable AI technique that identifies and highlights the most influential regions in medical images contributing to model predictions. This approach enhances both transparency and interpretability, which are critical factors in medical decision-making systems.

By visually mapping model attention onto the original image, the system enables users to understand how predictions are formed, thereby improving trust in AI systems and supporting clinical validation. This is especially valuable for medical professionals, researchers, and students who rely on clear and explainable outputs.

Instead of functioning as a black-box model, Aura AI provides visual evidence for each prediction, allowing users to verify and interpret the model’s reasoning process. This significantly improves confidence in AI-assisted diagnostics and enhances its educational value in real-world applications.

ResNet Architecture

The Residual Network (ResNet) family, developed by Kaiming He and his team, addresses a major challenge in deep learning known as the vanishing gradient problem, where deeper neural networks tend to perform worse due to training instability. To solve this, ResNet introduced residual (skip) connections, allowing the input of a layer to be directly added to its output.

Instead of learning a direct mapping H(x), the network learns a residual function defined as F(x) = H(x) − x, resulting in the final output y = F(x) + x. This design improves training stability and enables the construction of very deep networks without degradation in performance.

ResNet-18 Architecture

ResNet-18 is a relatively lightweight version of the ResNet family, designed for computational efficiency and faster training. It takes an input image of size 224 × 224 × 3 and begins with a 7×7 convolution layer followed by a max-pooling layer.

The architecture is built using basic residual blocks, each consisting of two 3×3 convolution layers along with an identity skip connection. These blocks are organized into multiple stages, where feature depth increases while spatial dimensions decrease.

In total, ResNet-18 contains 18 layers, including convolutional and fully connected layers. Due to its smaller size, it is faster, less prone to overfitting, and suitable for smaller datasets or environments with limited computational power.

ResNet-101 Architecture

ResNet-101 is a much deeper network designed for complex feature extraction. While it shares a similar initial structure, it differs in the use of bottleneck residual blocks.

Each bottleneck block consists of three layers: a 1×1 convolution for dimensionality reduction, a 3×3 convolution for feature extraction, and another 1×1 convolution to restore dimensionality. This design improves efficiency while enabling very deep architectures.

The network is organized into stages with multiple bottleneck blocks, resulting in a total of 101 layers. This depth allows the model to learn fine-grained and complex features, making it highly effective for tasks such as medical image analysis. However, it requires higher computational resources and longer training time.

Key Differences

The main difference between ResNet-18 and ResNet-101 lies in their depth and complexity. ResNet-18 uses basic residual blocks, making it faster and more efficient but less capable of capturing highly complex patterns.

In contrast, ResNet-101 uses bottleneck blocks and a much deeper architecture, allowing it to learn more detailed features at the cost of increased computational demand. Therefore, ResNet-18 is suitable for lightweight applications, while ResNet-101 is preferred for high-accuracy tasks where computational resources are available.

Grad-CAM: Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping

Grad-CAM is a visualization technique for understanding how convolutional neural networks make decisions. It identifies which regions of an input image most influence predictions for a particular class. This interpretability is especially critical in medical imaging, where understanding model reasoning is as important as the prediction itself.

How Grad-CAM Works

Grad-CAM leverages gradients flowing into the final convolutional layer. When an image passes through the model, each class receives a prediction score. To understand a specific prediction, Grad-CAM computes the gradient of the target class score with respect to the feature maps of the final convolutional layer.

These gradients capture the importance of each feature map for the particular class. The gradients are globally averaged to obtain weights representing each feature map's contribution to the final prediction. Feature maps are then weighted and summed to create a coarse localization map highlighting influential regions.

Visualization and Interpretation

After generating the activation map, a ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) function removes negative values, retaining only features that positively contribute. The resulting heatmap is upsampled to match the original image size and overlaid with warm colors indicating highly important regions and cool colors showing less relevant areas.

  • Red and warm colors: regions with strong influence on the prediction
  • Blue and cool colors: regions with minimal influence
  • Visual overlay shows exactly where the model focused its attention

Applications in Medical Diagnosis

In medical diagnosis systems, Grad-CAM verifies that models focus on clinically relevant regions rather than spurious patterns. For example, in tumor detection, it confirms the model attends to the actual tumor region rather than background features. This validation helps researchers and practitioners verify meaningful learning and builds confidence in model predictions.

Advantages and Limitations

  • Applicable across diverse CNN architectures without major modifications
  • Provides interpretability and transparency for clinical validation
  • Enhances trust and acceptance of AI systems in healthcare
  • Heatmaps are relatively coarse and may lack pixel-level precision
  • Effectiveness depends heavily on the choice of convolutional layer
  • Incorrect layer selection can lead to misleading interpretations

Model Training and Optimization

The model training phase of the Aura AI system is built upon transfer learning, a widely adopted approach in modern deep learning workflows. Instead of training neural networks from scratch, pretrained models—originally trained on large-scale datasets such as ImageNet—are fine-tuned to perform specialized medical imaging tasks. This approach significantly reduces training time while improving performance, especially when working with limited medical datasets.

Two primary architectures were utilized for different tasks. The ResNet-18 model was selected for histopathology image classification due to its lightweight structure and efficiency. In contrast, the deeper ResNet-101 was employed for MRI-based classification, where capturing complex spatial features is essential for accurate predictions.

To optimize performance and computational efficiency, selective layer freezing was implemented. In this process, earlier layers of the pretrained network—responsible for general feature extraction—are kept unchanged, while later layers are fine-tuned for domain-specific learning. This strategy reduces computational cost and helps prevent overfitting, while fine-tuning improves the model’s ability to generalize across unseen data.

Training was conducted using GPU acceleration, specifically NVIDIA T4 GPUs available on Kaggle, ensuring faster computation and scalability. This setup enables the system to handle large datasets efficiently while maintaining practical training times for real-world applications.

System Architecture

The Aura AI system is designed with a clean modular architecture that emphasizes scalability, maintainability, and extensibility. The backend is developed using the Flask framework, structured with modular Blueprints to separate functionalities into independent components. This approach ensures that each feature—such as image processing, model inference, and user interaction—operates in an isolated yet coordinated manner.

The project follows a well-organized directory structure, with clearly defined folders for trained models, utility scripts, templates, and static assets. Uploaded images and generated outputs are handled through dedicated storage directories, ensuring efficient file management and smooth processing workflows.

Additionally, the system integrates Docker containerization, enabling consistent deployment across development and production environments. By encapsulating dependencies and configurations within containers, the platform minimizes compatibility issues and simplifies deployment processes.

This architectural design allows for seamless integration of new modules and features without disrupting existing components. As a result, the system remains future-ready, flexible, and capable of adapting to evolving requirements in AI-driven medical applications.

Deployment and Execution

Aura AI supports flexible deployment options, allowing it to run both on local systems and cloud platforms. For local deployment, users must first create a virtual environment to isolate project dependencies. After installing the required libraries, trained model files are placed in designated directories, and environment variables are configured accordingly.

Once the setup is complete, the application can be executed using the following command: 

python run.py

The system is designed to support both CPU and GPU-based inference. While CPU execution ensures broader compatibility, GPU acceleration significantly improves performance, particularly for large-scale image processing tasks.

This dual compatibility enables the platform to adapt to different hardware configurations, making it accessible and efficient for a wide range of users, from basic systems to high-performance computing environments.

Features

Aura AI offers a comprehensive feature set designed to enhance both usability and functionality. One of its key strengths is the ability to perform multi-cancer detection within a single unified platform, supporting both histopathology and MRI image analysis for diverse diagnostic applications.

The system includes batch image processing, allowing users to analyze multiple images simultaneously, thereby improving efficiency and workflow speed. For interpretability, it integrates Grad-CAM (visual explanation technique), which highlights important regions in images that influence model predictions.

Aura AI features a clean and intuitive user interface, making it accessible even to non-technical users. Additionally, Dockerized deployment ensures easy installation and execution across different environments, eliminating complex configuration requirements.

Limitations

Despite its advanced capabilities, Aura AI has several important limitations that must be considered. The system is not intended for clinical diagnosis and should be used only as a research or assistive tool, rather than a replacement for medical professionals.

The system’s performance is highly dependent on the quality and diversity of training data. Poor or biased datasets can lead to inaccurate predictions. Additionally, trained model files are not included in the repository, requiring users to obtain or train them separately.

Aura AI is currently limited to specific cancer types, restricting its broader applicability in medical diagnostics. These limitations highlight the need for further validation, testing, and regulatory approval before real-world clinical deployment.
